    <span>01</span>
    <h2>From useful to reliable</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="about-relaxed-prose">
    <p>I spend much of my time thinking about AI safety through uncertainty quantification and management. AI can already do impressive things, but usefulness is not the same as reliability.</p>
    <p>I believe that managing uncertainty is what lets AI move from something people use to something they can rely on, in everyday life and even more so in medicine and other high-stakes settings. A dependable system should recognize weak evidence, express uncertainty meaningfully and change its behavior accordingly.</p>
    <p>That requires more than a confidence score added at the end. It means designing risk controls around decisions, multi-step workflows, changing conditions and the feedback produced by the system itself.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<p class="about-relaxed-thesis"><span>For me, uncertainty is not a warning attached to an answer.</span><span>It is part of how an intelligent system should decide what to do.</span></p>

    <h2>Experience, put to work</h2>

This can mean reviewing architecture, assumptions and technical risk; structuring a research program; planning a research-to-product transition; or providing ongoing scientific and technical guidance.</p>

  <div class="about-relaxed-section-heading">
    <span>03</span>
    <h2>Foundation Models</h2>
    <p>Beyond language</p>
  </div>
  <div class="about-relaxed-prose">
    <p>The success of foundation models for text raises a broader question: what actually made that success possible, and how much of it can travel to other domains?</p>
    <p>I am interested in representations that can be reused across contexts and recombined in unfamiliar ways. This is the kind of structure that could support foundation models for vision, robotics and systems that must perceive, act and learn over time.</p>
  </div>
</section>

    <h2>Research, then reality</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="about-relaxed-prose">
    <p>I enjoy theory, but I do not see research as finished when the paper is written. I like following the path from a strong question to an experiment, a robust software architecture and, eventually, a capability or product that matters.</p>

    <span aria-label="An open question">??</span>
    <h2>Natural Intelligence</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="about-relaxed-prose">
    <p>Behind these questions sits an even older one: what is intelligence? My interest reaches beyond artificial intelligence to natural intelligence: how abstractions are formed and recursively reused, whether they help explain generalization, and what they reveal about the computational limits of current models.</p>
    <p>I am interested in whether those principles can be made computational, not by imitating the surface behavior of intelligence, but by identifying the mechanisms that make abstraction, transfer and continued learning possible.</p>
